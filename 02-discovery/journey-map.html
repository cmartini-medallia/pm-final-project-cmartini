RouteLogic Velocity: Future-State Dispatch Journey

Persona: the live-ops dispatcher

Role
A dispatcher coordinating a regional fleet in real time, day or night shift, at a 3PL or enterprise account.
Goal
Absorb same-day disruptions and keep every driver on the right stop from one board she can trust.
Friction
Reassignments take 8 to 15 minutes to reach drivers with no alert, and the board shows finished stops as in progress for up to an hour, so a WhatsApp group runs live operations.

Other end of the journey: the route driver, who receives the change and closes each stop. Evidence for this persona comes from UXR-02 and UXR-09 (dispatchers) plus the driver interviews that describe the handoff.

Future state: a driver calls out mid-shift

  1. 1

    See the live picture

    User action

    Opens dispatch view with live routes and exceptions only → spots the call-out immediately.

    Internal state

    Oriented. The board matches the road, so no WhatsApp check.

    Pain point addressed

    Board lags 20 to 60 minutes; core actions buried under unused features.

    BUG-2072, UXR-09, BUG-2079, UXR-04

    Replaces: scrolling the WhatsApp group to learn what actually happened

  2. 2

    Reassign in one move

    User action

    Moves remaining stops to a nearby driver in one screen → decision made in seconds.

    Internal state

    In control. No compliance or finance screens between her and the decision.

    Pain point addressed

    Core actions sit two to three levels deep after feature additions.

    BUG-2079, UXR-04, UXR-11

    Replaces: reassigning in RouteLogic, then repeating it by phone in case it never lands

  3. 3

    Driver gets it now

    User action

    Change pushes to driver with an alert; driver taps to accept → dispatcher sees confirmation.

    Internal state

    Relieved. She knows the driver has the new route, rather than hoping.

    Pain point addressed

    Reassignments take 8 to 15 minutes with no notification; drivers head the wrong way.

    BUG-2044 (Critical), UXR-02

    Replaces: the WhatsApp group as the real dispatch system

  4. 4

    Close the loop as it happens

    User action

    Driver marks delivered in one tap, even offline → board and reports update without backfill.

    Internal state

    Finished. Nothing to reconcile at the end of the shift.

    Pain point addressed

    Three-screen delivery confirmation, blank offline routes, slow status sync.

    BUG-2055, UXR-01, BUG-2050, BUG-2072

    Replaces: drivers texting completions for the dispatcher to log later

Competitive advantages over the manual workaround

  • One loop, not three channels

    The change, the driver's acknowledgment, and the stop status live in one place, replacing the WhatsApp group, phone calls, and texts.

  • The route changes, not just the chat

    A group message still leaves the driver to rework their route by hand. Here the new stops land in the driver's app with confirmation the dispatcher can see.

  • The record writes itself

    Events enter RouteLogic as they happen, so the reporting buyers paid for (UXR-10) is complete and on time instead of eroded by off-platform work.

Conditions and evidence gaps

  • The bar is WhatsApp speed. Stage 3 only beats the habit if sync is near-instant, so fixing BUG-2044, BUG-2031 (crash) and BUG-2050 (offline) is the entry ticket; simplifying screens alone will not move users off the group chat.
  • Dispatcher evidence is thin: two interviews (UXR-02, UXR-09). Contextual inquiry with dispatchers during live disruptions is the next research step.
  • End-of-shift backfill is inferred from drivers texting completions (UXR-01), not observed directly.
  • The Module 1 hypothesis of spreadsheets, whiteboards, and Google Maps did not appear in the research; the observed workarounds are WhatsApp, texts, calls, screenshots, and paper manifests.
